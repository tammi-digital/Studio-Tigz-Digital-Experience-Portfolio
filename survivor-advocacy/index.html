When the pattern has a name

Narcissistic abuse can be confusing because the harm is often hidden between moments of charm.

Narcissistic abuse is a phrase survivors use to describe a repeated pattern of manipulation, entitlement, control, and emotional harm. It does not require a clinical diagnosis to be real. This page focuses on behaviors and their impact, not labeling another person.

Love bombingIntense attention, promises, or fast intimacy can create attachment before control appears.
GaslightingDenial, rewriting events, or calling you “too sensitive” can make you doubt your memory and judgment.
Isolation and controlMonitoring money, time, friendships, clothing, or communication can slowly shrink a survivor’s choices.
Devaluation and blameWarmth may shift into criticism, projection, silent treatment, or punishment when needs are expressed.
Intermittent reinforcementCycles of harm followed by apologies or affection can make leaving feel emotionally and practically difficult.
Smear campaignsPrivate stories may be distorted publicly, leaving survivors afraid they will not be believed.
